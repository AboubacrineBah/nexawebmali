NexaStock

Demo project and In production

A stock-management product designed to help Malian retailers, distributors, and workshops track what is available, what moves, and what needs replenishment.

Client:
NexaWeb Mali (internal product).
Industry:
Retail, distribution, and workshops.
Year:
2026.
Duration:
3 months.
Roles:
Requirements, UX/UI design, back-end and front-end development, testing, documentation, and deployment.
Technologies:
Laravel, PHP, MySQL, Bootstrap 5, Vite, PHPUnit.

Context

Many small businesses in Mali track inventory in notebooks or spreadsheets, or use tools that do not provide a clear enough history. This leads to stock shortages discovered too late, discrepancies that are hard to explain, and little visibility into stock movements. NexaStock addresses this need with a simple dashboard showing what is available, what is moving, and what needs replenishment. The MVP scope is to be confirmed with 3 to 5 pilot businesses in Bamako.

Challenges

Reliable, traceable quantities: every balance must be explained by validated movements, with no silent data changes.

Separating each company's data: no customer should be able to read or change another company's data, including by changing a URL identifier or exporting data.

Everyday, mobile-first use: quick entry in a few clicks, a lightweight interface despite network or power outages, and a target of under 3 seconds for common pages.

A manageable MVP for a small team: accounting, cash management, a native app, offline mode, and advanced batch management are out of scope for the first release.

Solutions

Laravel (MVC) and MySQL: 9 business entities (company, user, role, item, category, location, movement, supplier, and inventory) with atomic transactions for movements and transfers.

Immutable movements: receipts, issues, adjustments, and transfers record quantity, date, reason, and author. Corrections and cancellations create linked compensating movements, while an audit log records sensitive actions.

Server-side access controls: 4 roles (NexaWeb administrator, company administrator, stock manager, and operator), secure sessions, CSRF protection, login rate limiting, hashed passwords, and HTTPS in production.

Guided workflows and a product-specific interface: receiving, issuing (negative stock is rejected), inventory with justified adjustments, an exportable replenishment list, and CSV import with an error preview. The responsive Blade interface has a dedicated NexaStock logo and palette; alerts use text and icons as well as color.

Operations and quality: development, staging, and production environments, secrets kept out of the code repository, automated backups with documented restore tests, and a RUN service for updates and support.

Results

  • An MVP scope defined: 11 modules, including 8 essential ones (access, items, movements, alerts, inventory, reports, audit log, and company workspaces)
  • 5 main workflows described across 7 screen groups (receiving, issuing, inventory, replenishment, and catalog import)
  • 11 verifiable acceptance criteria, including company isolation, traceable corrections, mobile use, and backup restoration
  • User validation planned: 15 interviews in Bamako, 3 to 5 pilots, and a target of entering at least 80% of routine operations in the tool
  • Load time, Lighthouse scores, pilot retention, and acceptance results are to be measured after the pilot
